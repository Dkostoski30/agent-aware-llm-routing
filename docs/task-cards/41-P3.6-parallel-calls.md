# P3.6: Parallel calls

Parallel calls come from the replayer's fan-out, not the demo agent (decided 2026-11-01). The parallel calls of one step reach the picker at almost the same moment, faster than a 50 ms metrics refresh can show them in the queue, so the plugin has to count its own calls in flight per group. Runs on sim (decided 2026-11-09).

Decided 2026-11-11 with the user, planning P3.6:
- **The guard first.** With both rules on, the overload guard judges a group's call first; only a call it leaves on its pinned pod goes to the parallel rule. The done-when runs with the guard off, so the rule is shown alone. With today's first guesses (guard over 1, parallel over 2), while the guard is on, a group of 2 finds its pod full only when every pod's queue is over 1, since the guard spills first whenever another pod is at or under 1. (Planning said "never", which the fresh-eyes review corrected: the guard leaves a call alone when no other pod has room.) That is a point for P3.7, which measures both thresholds.
- **Count in Filter.** The count rises in Filter, in one step with the room check under one lock, not in PreRequest. The replayer sends a group's calls at once (`asyncio.gather`), so the second call's Filter would otherwise run before the first call's PreRequest and see nothing in flight.
- **Spill only to room.** A spill goes only to the other pods at or under the threshold; with none, the call stays, as the guard's spill does.
- **No rule for an unpinned group.** The card's "a group with no pin yet binds at its first call" is dropped. The replayer never fans out step 1 (`plan.py`), so a group lacks a live pin only after its TTL ran out or its pod left (P3.4). Its calls then go where the scorers send them, and calls that arrive together may land on two pods: a stated limit.

Result: `experiments/log.md`, 2026-11-12, green. The rule held in both replays: every spill was over the threshold, and each run's next call went back to its pinned pod. steady-fan-out spilled 1 call against this card's prediction of 0, since its pod had 2 calls waiting plus 1 in flight. The prediction below is left as written.

Claude's choices, stated to the user:
- The count is per (run, group, pinned pod).
- `parallel_spill` on with affinity off is refused, as the guard is (`routing-values.sh`, `experiment.py`).
- `parallel_queue_threshold` is a whole number and belongs to the profile, like the overload limits.
- SEED=6, so burst-fan-out has the runs and arrivals of P3.5's burst.

# P3.6: Parallel calls

Goal:
Calls of one x-parallel-group stay on the run's pod while it has room, and spill to another pod only when it doesn't. A spilled
parallel call leaves the pin where it is.

Depends on:
P3.5

Files allowed:
gateway/runrouter/, config/real.yaml, config/real-plain.yaml, config/sim.yaml, config/demo.yaml (routing section, the burst
with fan-out pattern), infra/routing-values.sh, loadgen/tests/ (the pattern's tests only), experiments/log.md, PLAN.md (P3.6 row),
docs/task-cards/41-P3.6-parallel-calls.md
Added 2026-11-11 (planning): loadgen/load_generator/experiment.py and loadgen/tests/test_experiment.py (the threshold is the
profile's, parallel_spill needs affinity), loadgen/tests/test_routing_values.py (its sample profile gains the threshold),
experiments/results/replays/parallel-*-6.jsonl and experiments/results/parallel-*-6-picker.log (the done-when's replays and
picker logs)

Interfaces / outputs:
- With routing.parallel_spill, for a call with x-parallel-group whose run has a live pin, and that the overload guard (if on)
  left on the pinned pod: room on the pinned pod = its waiting queue plus the group's calls already sent to it and not ended,
  at or under routing.parallel_queue_threshold. Room: the pinned pod, and the call is counted there (parallel-stay). No room:
  the other candidates at or under the threshold (parallel-spill); the pin stays and its TTL is renewed. No other pod at or
  under it: the pinned pod, counted (parallel-stay).
- The in-flight count per (run, group, pinned pod) rises in Filter, under the same lock as the room check, and falls in
  ResponseBody at end of stream, whose hook also runs when a call that got a pod fails, is cancelled or its client leaves
  (llm-d-router v0.11.0 handlers/server.go), so a failed call does not hold its count. A spilled call is counted nowhere,
  so when a group's first call finds no room, its second spills too.
- A group whose run has no live pin gets no rule (no pin to keep it on).
- parallel_spill on with affinity off is refused by infra/routing-values.sh and experiment.py.
- routing.parallel_queue_threshold, first guess 2 (a fan-out of 2, on a simulated pod running 2 at once), the profile's alone.
- JSON log lines with reason parallel-stay and parallel-spill, with run-id, step, group, pod, queue (the pinned pod's) and
  inFlight (the group's calls on it before this one); a spill also has from. The counter of P3.5 gains both.
- replay.patterns.burst-fan-out in every profile, the same in all (P2.3's comment promised it): burst's arrivals, fan_out share 0.5, k 2.

Notes:
Tests first, table-driven: the group stays while it has room; the call that finds it full spills; another group of the run
counts apart; a call's end frees room, also a failed one, and a chunk before the end does not; a call ended twice frees once;
a spilled call does not move the pin and is not counted, so a group whose first call spills spills its second too; only pods
with room are offered; the guard decides first, and with every pod over its threshold leaves the call to the parallel rule;
flag off changes nothing; a group with no live pin gets no rule. Upstream's RunningRequestsSize is ignored here on purpose: it lags the
same as the queue. Being a ResponseBody plugin makes the picker run its body plugins for every chunk of every call, which it
skips when none is registered: a small cost per chunk.
