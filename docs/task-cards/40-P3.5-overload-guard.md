# P3.5: Overload guard

The picker reads each pod's `WaitingQueueSize` from vLLM's `vllm:num_requests_waiting`, refreshed every 50 ms by default (`--refresh-metrics-interval`, read in v0.11.0). On a real pod this queue also holds calls waiting for KV blocks, the waiting behind every p95 miss in P2.9.

Runs on sim (decided 2026-11-09): this task proves behaviour, and P3.7 makes the latency claim.

Decided 2026-11-11 with the user, planning P3.5: four more files (below); a remap restarts the pod's timer, so the runs
pinned to an overloaded pod leave one per window; the guard watches the queue only, and KV pressure is a stated limit;
`overload_guard` on with affinity off is refused before helm runs. Result: `experiments/log.md`, 2026-11-11 (spill shown
on sim, remap by the unit tests only: no overload lasted 10 s).

# P3.5: Overload guard

Goal:
A pinned run stays pinned unless its pod is overloaded: over the queue threshold one call spills to another pod and the pin
stays; only sustained overload moves the pin. A short spike never moves it.

Depends on:
P3.3

Files allowed:
gateway/runrouter/, config/real.yaml, config/real-plain.yaml, config/sim.yaml, config/demo.yaml (routing section),
infra/routing-values.sh, experiments/log.md, PLAN.md (P3.5 row), docs/task-cards/40-P3.5-overload-guard.md
Added 2026-11-11 (planning): gateway/go.mod (prometheus/client_golang v1.23.2 from indirect to direct, for the counter;
no new module), loadgen/load_generator/experiment.py and loadgen/tests/test_experiment.py (the two limits are no rules),
loadgen/tests/test_routing_values.py (its sample profile gains the limits), experiments/results/replays/overload-*.jsonl
and experiments/results/overload-*-picker.log (the done-when's replays and picker logs)

Interfaces / outputs:
- With routing.overload_guard: pinned pod's queue over routing.overload_queue_threshold (strictly greater), and another
  candidate at or under it: the candidates at or under it, without the pinned pod (spill); the pin stays and its TTL is
  renewed. Every candidate over it: the pinned pod, since moving gains nothing and loses the cache.
- Over the threshold without a break for routing.overload_sustained_seconds: the call goes to the candidates at or under
  it, without the pinned pod, and PreRequest moves the pin to the pod chosen (remap). Not all candidates: the prefix
  scorer would send the call back to the pinned pod. Back at or under the threshold: the timer resets. A remap restarts
  the pod's timer, so the runs pinned there leave one per window (decided 2026-11-11).
- The timer is per pod. Every call through the filter looks at every candidate's queue (WaitingQueueSize, read every
  50 ms): the first look over the threshold starts it, the first at or under drops it, as does a pod leaving the pool.
- The guard acts on pinned runs only: routing-values.sh and experiment.py refuse overload_guard on with affinity off
  (decided 2026-11-11). Both limits are whole numbers and the profile's alone, like run_ttl_seconds: a variant sets rules
  only.
- routing.overload_queue_threshold, first guess 1 (one call already waiting for a slot or KV blocks);
  routing.overload_sustained_seconds, first guess 10 (several p50 calls of 2.3 s, P2.9, so one long call is not "sustained").
  Both commented as first guesses; P3.7 measures them.
- JSON log lines with reason spill and remap, with run-id, step, pod, from (the overloaded pod) and queue (its waiting
  calls); a remap also has overloadedSeconds. A counter thesis_picker_decisions_total{decision} (first, pinned,
  remapped, spill, remap) on controller-runtime's registry, which the picker serves on its metrics port (9090, behind
  the picker's metrics auth); a unit test reads it, the done-when reads the logs.

Notes:
Tests first, table-driven with a fake clock: under the threshold stays; a spike spills and keeps the pin, and the next call
returns; sustained remaps once; a dip resets the timer; all pods overloaded stays; guard off never spills. Under sustained
overload a spill loses the cache but prefill is cheap (0.146 ms per uncached token against seconds in a queue, P2.8c), so
spilling is expected to help. Queue only, decided 2026-11-11: P3.3's bad case was KV pressure (two runs pinned to one
pod, 10-11k tokens against 4,384), which on a real pod shows in this queue only once calls wait for KV blocks; the sim
never makes a call wait for KV blocks, so a KV rule could not be shown working here. A stated limit for P3.7 and ch. 6.
