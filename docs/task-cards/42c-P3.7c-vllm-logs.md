Decided 2026-11-13 with the user, after P3.7b's routing-context runs stopped on pod stalls. Both stalled pods' vLLM logs
survived a reboot by luck (status Unknown, `kubectl logs` still answered); they showed the "stall" is starvation, not a
freeze. On `vllm-6786987f8c-6dhf6`, 10:20-10:22 UTC: 0 prompt and generation tokens in some 10 s intervals and no stats
line in others (the engine stuck in one long step), 1 call running and 6-9 waiting, while the other pod,
`vllm-6786987f8c-jjnph`, made about 630 prompt tokens/s every 10 s. No preemption line in either log. Both pods ran about
one call at a time with KV use at 50-80% and 3-12 waiting: a context of about 2.5k tokens fills half of the 4,384-token
cache, so the next call is not admitted. Two vLLM processes time-slice the one GPU, and here one got almost none of it
for up to a minute; a prefill of about 0.6 s took 30 s or more, vLLM counts prompt tokens when a prefill ends, so
Prometheus saw no tokens and calls behind it hit the 120 s timeout. Why one process starves, and whether a routing rule
makes it likelier, needs more stalls with logs: this card keeps them.

Claude's choices, stated in the plan and approved ('yes'): whole logs, not filtered (about 1.3 MB per pod, 96% the
picker's /metrics scrapes, as large as a picker log); `kubectl logs --timestamps`, so the access lines line up with the
stats lines; real profiles only (simulated pods share no GPU); taken after the picker log and before the stall check and
the next restart; the fake cluster records the takes apart from `asked`, as the GPU log's are, so no existing test
changes. The two salvaged logs are in `experiments/results/routing-context-pod-stall/` with the stalled runs' other files
